One German in five is sixty-seven or over; by twenty thirty-five, one in four. More patients, fewer people paying in. Health already costs more than five hundred billion euros a year.

Contributions for health, pensions, care and unemployment take about forty-two percent of a typical salary. This year the public insurers' spending grew seven percent; their income, four. Without action, the government projected a gap of tens of billions a year by twenty thirty — and in July it passed a law to close it.

And the economy that pays the bills has stalled: three years of almost no growth, energy-hungry industry down nearly a fifth since twenty twenty-one, Chinese factories competing with German ones. Growth is expected to pick up a little, then fade again.

Not a collapse. But money will be tight for a long time — and when money is tight, people look for someone to blame.
