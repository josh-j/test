Follow the decisions upward. Berlin writes the rules: the single price, the readiness payment, a fund of up to fifty billion euros to help hospitals restructure. It argues there are too many beds and too much care done too rarely to be done well.

The states plan the hospitals and are meant to pay for the buildings. Several pushed back on Berlin's reform as an intrusion into their planning.

Berlin points to the states. The states point to Berlin. Both point to the negotiators. The negotiators point to the law.

Step back and you see why. The money for buildings and the money for treatment come through different pipes. Rules come from one capital, planning from another, prices from a table where no side can decide alone. No one owns a patient's whole journey — from the doctor's practice, to the operating theatre, to the bed at home.

In a machine like this, blame never has to land anywhere.
