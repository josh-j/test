# The Hospital That Must Change Shape · Narration script (main cut, 5:50)

A paste-ready script for a narrator or a text-to-speech service. Each scene is also saved as a plain-text file (`scene-01.txt` … `scene-08.txt`) for direct pasting. Numbers are spelled out so the voice reads them correctly. Generate one audio file per scene and name it `scene-01.mp3` and so on.

**Length:** about 625 words in total, at a target pace of about 125 words per minute, including pauses.

---

## Voice brief

Paste this into a voice-design or voice-search tool, or give it to a human narrator:

> Warm, older British male narrator, late sixties to seventies. Unhurried and softly spoken, with a gently curious, observational tone — like a natural-history documentary. Understated, with quiet wonder and occasional dry warmth. Never dramatic, never salesy. Clear received pronunciation, slight gravel, close-mic intimacy.

**Do not** clone, imitate or prompt for any real person's voice (for example, "sounds like David Attenborough"). It breaks the services' terms and carries legal risk. Describe qualities only.

### Text-to-speech settings (starting points)

- **ElevenLabs:**
  - Use the most expressive model (v3).
  - Stability: about 45–55%. Lower is more expressive, higher is more consistent.
  - Style exaggeration: low, about 0–15%. Speaker boost: on.
  - Generate paragraph by paragraph, then keep the best take of each.
- **Gemini TTS:** put the voice brief and the scene direction in the prompt. Example: *"Read in the voice described: hushed, unhurried, pausing after each paragraph."*
- **Pauses:** add exact pauses in editing rather than relying on punctuation, so the timing is controlled. A blank line in the script means a pause of about 1 second, or the length given in the scene notes.
- **Pace check:** if a scene runs longer than its target, slow the visuals, don't rush the voice.

### Pronunciation

- *rota*: ROH-tuh (British).
- *euros*: YOOR-ohs.
- *Scandinavia*: standard English.
- No German place names are spoken. Saarbrücken appears only on screen.

## Scene 1 · The habitat
**00:00 – 00:25** · 39 words · file: `scene-01.mp3`

**Tone:** Narration starts at 0:05, after the opening card. Hushed, close to the microphone, as if not to wake the building.

**Direction:**
- Pause 1 second after "sleeps."
- "the part that waits": slower, almost tender.
- A 2-second pause before "Here, in a quiet corner…"
- End lightly: "a very *small* operation" is curious, with a slight smile.

**Read:**

```
At night, most of this hospital sleeps.

One part never does — the part that waits.

Here, in a quiet corner of Germany, a hospital is being asked to change its shape.

It begins with a very small operation.
```

## Scene 2 · One price, two paths
**00:25 – 01:13** · 89 words · file: `scene-02.mp3`

**Tone:** Plain and explanatory, like pointing out a creature's habits.

**Direction:**
- A light, factual "In Scandinavia and Britain… In Germany…", with no judgement.
- Mark the turn: "Now the rules have changed." Short pause before and after.
- "Who is fit to go home remains a doctor's decision" is reassuring and firm.

**Read:**

```
A groin hernia is a weak point in the wall of the body. Most patients need no night in hospital to repair it.

In Scandinavia and Britain, most go home the same day. In Germany, most have stayed the night.

Now the rules have changed. For a simple case, the hospital is paid one fixed price — whether the patient goes home that evening, or stays until morning.

Who is fit to go home remains a doctor's decision. The price simply stops paying for nights that are not needed.
```

## Scene 3 · The case for change
**01:13 – 01:44** · 56 words · file: `scene-03.mp3`

**Tone:** Even-handed. You are reporting the insurers' view fairly, not arguing it.

**Direction:**
- "any country in the European Union but one": keep it light; no stress on "one".
- "Yet its people live no longer…": a gentle, raised-eyebrow understatement.
- A 1.5-second pause, then quietly: "It is the cure."

**Read:**

```
To the insurers, this change is overdue. Germany has more hospital beds per person than any country in the European Union but one. It spends more on health per person than any of them. Yet its people live no longer than the European average.

Some shrinking, they argue, is not the illness.

It is the cure.
```

## Scene 4 · The weight passes down
**01:44 – 02:36** · 97 words · file: `scene-04.mp3`

**Tone:** Wider and slower, a bigger-picture voice. Calm, not alarmed.

**Direction:**
- Say the numbers plainly: "one in five… one in four".
- "Each decision is reasonable." is said with sympathy. Pause 1 second after.
- The last line descends in pitch, settling at the bottom like the weight.

**Read:**

```
The pressure runs deeper. One German in five is sixty-seven or over; by twenty thirty-five, it will be one in four. Fewer workers pay in. More patients need care.

And the economy that paid the bills is losing power — the cheap Russian gas is gone, and Chinese factories now compete with German ones.

So the weight passes down. Government limits what it adds. Insurers fix their prices. The regions delay new buildings. Each decision is reasonable.

And near the bottom sits a hospital that cannot pass on its night shift, its emergency team, or its walls.
```

## Scene 5 · The empty bed, and the part that cannot sleep
**02:36 – 03:24** · 89 words · file: `scene-05.mp3`

**Tone:** Observational and a little wry on "On paper".

**Direction:**
- "But the nurse is still on the rota.": a small, knowing pause before it.
- "the part that cannot sleep" echoes scene 1, so use the same hushed tone.
- "twenty thirty" against "already here": let the contrast land. Pause 1.5 seconds before the last line.

**Read:**

```
When a patient goes home, a bed empties. On paper, a few hundred euros vanish from the bill. But the nurse is still on the rota. Little money is saved — until the hospital truly changes what it spends.

And there is the part that cannot sleep. For years, routine operations helped to pay for it.

Two German hospitals in three are losing money. A payment just for staying ready is now law — but it arrives in full only in twenty thirty.

The new price is already here.
```

## Scene 6 · Changing shape
**03:24 – 04:15** · 97 words · file: `scene-06.mp3`

**Tone:** Practical, quietly hopeful. The most forward-looking scene.

**Direction:**
- "Who pays for a new shape?": a genuine question, not rhetorical.
- "Once the money is confirmed": a slight emphasis on *once*. It is not yet confirmed.
- The final list: steady rhythm, no rush.

**Read:**

```
Who pays for a new shape? In Germany, the state pays for buildings; the insurers pay for treatment.

Here, the state has set aside eighty-five million euros for a new campus — with an outpatient theatre where hospital surgeons and local doctors will work side by side. Once the money is confirmed, it could open in five or six years.

The hospitals that adapt well give day surgery its own space, decide before the day who goes home, and choose what to do with the empty bed: close it, or give it to someone who is waiting.
```

## Scene 7 · Beyond the door
**04:15 – 04:49** · 60 words · file: `scene-07.mp3`

**Tone:** The most intimate scene. Softer, slower, warmer.

**Direction:**
- Pause 1 second after "tonight."
- A 2-second pause before "It was the care." Say it almost under the breath.
- "a kitchen, a spare room, a daughter's evening": three images, each given a beat.

**Read:**

```
When our patient leaves, the care changes address. Someone must drive them home. Someone should be there tonight.

For those who live alone, or far away, or are frail, the night in hospital was never a habit.

It was the care.

Work that leaves the ward does not vanish. It arrives in a kitchen, a spare room, a daughter's evening.
```

## Scene 8 · What will tell us
**04:49 – 05:40** · 96 words · file: `scene-08.mp3`

**Tone:** Return to the opening register: night, stillness, reflection.

**Direction:**
- "No one in the chain chose this.": no blame in the voice.
- A 2-second pause before "It will be told by the patient…"
- Final phrase: "when they need to come back" trails gently. Hold the silence; the music carries the end card.

**Read:**

```
The rules around this hospital can change in a single year. Its body changes slowly — the rotas, the wards, the lights that stay on for an emergency that has not yet arrived.

No one in the chain chose this. Each simply passed on what it could not carry.

Whether this becomes a hospital that adapts, or one that merely shrinks, will not be told by the empty bed.

It will be told by the patient who recovers well at home — and by whether someone is still here, awake, when they need to come back.
```
