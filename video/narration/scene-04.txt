The pressure runs deeper. One German in five is sixty-seven or over; by twenty thirty-five, it will be one in four. Fewer workers pay in. More patients need care.

And the economy that paid the bills is losing power — the cheap Russian gas is gone, and Chinese factories now compete with German ones.

So the weight passes down. Government limits what it adds. Insurers fix their prices. The regions delay new buildings. Each decision is reasonable.

And near the bottom sits a hospital that cannot pass on its night shift, its emergency team, or its walls.
